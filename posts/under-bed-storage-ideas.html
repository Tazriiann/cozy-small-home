Under-Bed Storage Ideas for Small Bedrooms

These under-bed storage ideas for small bedrooms turn the biggest empty space in the room — the gap under your bed — into a closet, a linen shelf, and a shoe rack, without buying a single piece of furniture. Most beds hide about eight cubic feet of space that nobody uses, while the closet overflows and shoes pile up by the door, and the fix is simply to fill that gap on purpose. Every idea below costs $10 to $50, needs no tools, and moves with you when your lease ends. If the closet is overflowing too, these small closet organization ideas for renters double your hanging space with a second rod and slim hangers.

Under-Bed Storage Ideas: How Much Space Is Really Under a Bed?

Measure the gap before you buy anything, because most bed frames leave six to eight inches of clearance, a platform bed leaves four to six inches, and a bed on the floor leaves none at all. Measure the height, the width, and the depth of the gap, then buy bins that are at least one inch shorter than the height, so they slide in and out without catching on the frame. A standard twin bed fits two flat bins side by side, a queen fits three, and a king fits four, as long as each bin is no wider than 24 inches.

If your bed sits on the floor, add risers first, because bed risers lift the frame by five to eight inches and cost $15 to $25 for a set of four. Risers work under most wooden and metal frames, and they create the gap that makes every other idea in this guide possible. Check that the risers fit your bed legs and that the bed stays stable before you load the bins underneath.

What Should I Store Under the Bed?

Store the things you need a few times a month, not the things you need every day, because under-bed storage works best for seasonal clothes, spare linens, and shoes you rotate, and it fails for anything you reach for daily.

Off-season clothes in vacuum bags, spare sheets and towels, out-of-season shoes, and luggage all belong under the bed, while everyday clothes, daily shoes, and anything heavy belong in the closet or on a shelf. Keep a simple list taped inside the closet door of what lives under the bed, so you never forget what you stored there.

1. Flat Clear Bins With Lids — About $12 to $20 Each

A flat bin about 32 inches long, 16 inches wide, and 6 inches high holds a season of folded clothes or two sets of spare sheets, and a clear lid keeps out dust while letting you see what is inside. Choose bins with wheels or with a handle on the front, so they slide out easily on carpet or a wood floor.

2. Vacuum Storage Bags — About $10 to $15 for a Set

Vacuum bags shrink bulky sweaters, coats, and comforters to about half their size, and they slide under a low bed where a rigid bin will not fit. Squeeze the air out with a vacuum cleaner, seal the bag, and label it with the season and the contents.

3. Rolling Drawers — About $25 to $40 Each

A rolling drawer on wheels works like a dresser drawer that lives under the bed, and it suits shoes, folded clothes, and craft supplies you reach for weekly. Look for a drawer with a lid, so dust stays out, and with wheels that roll smoothly on your floor type.

4. A Bed Skirt or a Dust Ruffle — About $15 to $25

A bed skirt hides the bins underneath, so the bedroom looks tidy even when the storage is full. Choose a washable fabric in a color that matches your bedding, and make sure it is long enough to reach the floor without dragging.

How Do I Keep Under-Bed Storage Clean and Dry?

Use bins with lids, because an open bin under a bed collects dust, pet hair, and moisture, while a closed bin keeps everything clean for months. Wipe the floor under the bed every month, vacuum the bins when you pull them out.

Never store anything directly on the floor without a bin or a bag, because the floor under a bed is the dustiest spot in the room. If your bedroom is damp, add a small moisture absorber or a cedar block inside each bin, and check the bins every season for musty smells.

Label every bin on the front, not on the top, because you can read a front label without pulling the bin all the way out, and a top label is useless once the bin slides under the bed. Use a label maker, masking tape and a marker, or a luggage tag tied to the handle, and write the season and the contents in big letters you can read from across the room.

Under-Bed Storage Ideas: How Do I Store Shoes Under the Bed?

Store shoes in flat, lidded bins or in a rolling shoe organizer, because shoes left loose under a bed slide to the back, collect dust, and come out scuffed. A flat bin holds three to four pairs of flat shoes or two pairs of boots, and a rolling organizer with dividers keeps each pair separate so they do not crush each other. Keep the shoes you wear this week by the front door instead — see these small entryway ideas for apartments for a rack and tray that fit a narrow hall — and store the out-of-season pairs under the bed.

Clean shoes before you store them, because dirt and salt left on shoes will stain the bin and damage the leather or fabric while they sit for months. Stuff boots with rolled-up newspaper or a pool noodle cut to size, so they keep their shape, and add a small sachet of baking soda inside the bin to stop odors from building up.

How Do I Make Under-Bed Storage Work in a Shared Bedroom?

Give each person their own bins, because shared under-bed storage only works when nobody has to dig through someone else's things to find their own. Mark each bin with a name or a color, keep one side of the bed for each person, and agree on what lives under the bed before you buy the bins.

A couple sharing a queen bed can fit three bins — one each, plus one shared bin for spare linens — and a family sharing a room can add a second row of bins along the foot of the bed.

Do a seasonal swap together, because the swap is the moment when under-bed storage either stays organized or turns into a junk drawer. If the bedroom still feels crowded after the swap, our small bedroom ideas for renters show how to keep the sleep zone calm. Twice a year, pull every bin out, wipe the floor, check what still fits and what you still wear, and donate anything that has not been used in a year. The swap takes about an hour, and it keeps the space under the bed working instead of slowly filling up.

What Should I Buy First for Under-Bed Storage on a Budget?

Buy two flat bins with lids and a set of vacuum bags first, because those two buys cover seasonal clothes and spare linens for about $25 to $40 total, which is most of what a small bedroom needs to store.

The bins hold the folded clothes, the vacuum bags shrink the bulky coats and comforters, and both slide under a standard bed without any tools. Add bed risers first if your bed sits too low, then rolling drawers only if you reach for the stored items every week.

Measure the gap under your bed before you order anything, because a bin that is one inch too tall will not slide under the frame, and a bin that is too long will stick out past the foot of the bed. Keep receipts and packaging for a week, and buy from a store with easy returns, so a wrong size costs you a trip and not a wasted purchase.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it bad to store things under the bed?

Under-bed storage is fine as long as you use closed bins or bags, keep the floor clean, and store things you need a few times a month rather than every day. Dust and moisture are the only real risks, and a lidded bin solves both, as long as you wipe the floor under the bed once a month and check the bins every season for musty smells. These under-bed storage ideas only work with closed bins, because an open bin under a bed collects dust within weeks.

What is the best container for under-bed storage?

A flat, clear bin with a lid and a handle is usually best, because it slides in and out easily, keeps dust out, and lets you see what is inside without opening it. Vacuum bags work better for bulky coats and comforters, because they shrink to half their size and fit under a low bed where a rigid bin will not. Choose wheels for carpet and handles for wood floors.

How do I store clothes under the bed without them smelling musty?

Wash and fully dry clothes before you store them, use a closed bin or a sealed vacuum bag, and add a cedar block or a small moisture absorber inside the bin. Never store damp clothes, because moisture trapped in a closed bin turns musty within weeks. Check the bins every season, air the contents out for a day, and wash anything that smells stale before you wear it.

Can I use under-bed storage with a platform bed?

Yes, if the platform leaves at least four inches of clearance, because flat bins and vacuum bags fit in a four-inch gap. Measure the gap first, and buy bins no taller than three inches so they slide freely. If the platform sits on the floor with no gap, add bed risers or switch to a storage bed with built-in drawers, which is designed for the same job.

How much can I fit under a queen bed?

A queen bed fits about three flat bins side by side, or about eight cubic feet of storage, which is roughly a season of folded clothes, two sets of spare sheets, and three to four pairs of out-of-season shoes. Measure your gap first, because the exact number depends on the height under your bed, and a taller gap with bed risers can double the space.
