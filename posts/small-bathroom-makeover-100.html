Small Bathroom Makeover Under $100

This small bathroom makeover under $100 proves a tiny rental bathroom can feel new without a contractor, a new vanity, or any permanent changes. A small bathroom makeover at this price works by swapping the things your eyes land on first: the mirror, the lighting, the hardware, and the clutter on the counter. Every idea below is renter-friendly, costs $10 to $40, and fits into one weekend, so you can start Friday evening and shower in a new-feeling bathroom by Sunday.

What Should You Change First in a Small Bathroom Makeover?

Change the mirror and the lighting first, because a bigger mirror and a warmer bulb change how the whole room looks for about $30 total. A plain builder mirror makes a bathroom feel flat, while a framed mirror with a warm bulb makes it feel finished and twice as bright.

Start by taking everything off the counter, the toilet tank, and the shower ledge, and put back only what you use every day. Move the rest into a bin inside the cabinet or onto a rolling cart. This free step alone makes the room feel larger, and it shows you what storage you actually need before you spend anything. For cheap storage that fits this budget, see these small bathroom storage ideas under $25, which cover shower caddies, bins, and towel bars.

Next, look at the mirror. If your bathroom has a plain flat mirror glued to the wall, frame it with peel-and-stick trim or lean a framed mirror against the wall in front of it. A simple wood or black frame costs $15 to $30 at Target or Walmart, and it turns a builder mirror into a feature. If the mirror is small, replace it with a larger one, because a bigger mirror bounces more light and makes a tiny bathroom feel twice its size.

How Do You Do a Small Bathroom Makeover Under $100?

Do a small bathroom makeover under $100 by spending your money in this order: mirror, lighting, hardware, textiles, and storage, with about $20 left for paint or peel-and-stick touches. This order works because the mirror and light change the whole room, the hardware makes the vanity look new, and fresh towels and a shower curtain finish the job.

  • Framed mirror or mirror trim — $20. A larger framed mirror, or peel-and-stick trim around the existing mirror, is the single biggest visual change in the room.
  • Warm bulbs and a new light shade — $15. Swap cool white bulbs for warm white bulbs around 2700K, and replace a dated glass shade with a simple frosted one if your lease allows it.
  • New cabinet knobs and a towel ring — $18. Matching knobs in matte black or brushed nickel make a plain vanity look new, and they screw on with a screwdriver in minutes.
  • Shower curtain and bath mat — $25. A light, plain shower curtain and a washable bath mat in a soft color set the whole palette for the room.
  • Storage basket and tray — $12. A small basket for rolled towels and a tray for the soap and toothbrush holder keep the counter clear.
  • Peel-and-stick accents — $10. A strip of peel-and-stick tile or wallpaper behind the mirror adds color without paint or drilling.
  • Total: $100. If you already own a good mirror or shower curtain, move that money to a second basket or a small plant.

Keep the old hardware, bulbs, and shower curtain in a bag in the closet, because you will need to put the original pieces back when you move out. Take a photo of the bathroom before you start, so you remember exactly how it looked and can restore it in an hour on moving day.

Can You Paint a Small Rental Bathroom Without Losing Your Deposit?

Yes, if your lease allows it and you use the right approach, but most renters should skip paint and use peel-and-stick color instead. A small bathroom makeover under $100 rarely needs paint at all, because a new shower curtain, towels, and a peel-and-stick accent strip add the same color for less money and zero deposit risk.

If your landlord says yes, choose a light, warm white or pale gray paint made for bathrooms, because bathroom paint resists moisture and steam better than regular wall paint. A quart costs $15 to $20 and covers a small bathroom with two coats. Paint only the walls, never the tile or the tub, and keep the original paint can and color name so you can repaint before you move out.

If your landlord says no, or you do not want the hassle, use peel-and-stick wallpaper on one wall, or peel-and-stick tiles behind the mirror. These peel and stick wallpaper ideas for renters work well in bathrooms if you choose a moisture-resistant vinyl, and they peel off cleanly when you move. How Do I Make a Small Bathroom Feel Bigger on a Budget?

Make a small bathroom feel bigger by keeping surfaces light, adding one large mirror, and removing everything that does not need to be visible. Light towels, a clear glass shower curtain, and a pale bath mat reflect light, while a dark shower curtain and a crowded counter make the room feel smaller.

Swap a dark or patterned shower curtain for a clear or white one, because you can see the whole tub and the tile behind it, which makes the room read as one open space instead of two small ones. Choose a light bath mat that matches the towels, and fold the towels neatly instead of draping them over the shower rod. A small plant in a hanging planter adds life without taking counter space, and a eucalyptus bunch tied to the showerhead costs $8 and smells wonderful in the steam.

Lighting matters more than decoration here. A warm bulb makes skin and tile look better, and a small battery-powered sconce or a plug-in night light in a dark corner removes the shadows that make a tiny room feel cramped. What Are the Easiest Small Bathroom Makeover Swaps?

The easiest swaps are the ones that need only a screwdriver: cabinet knobs, the toilet seat, the showerhead, the faucet aerator, and the towel hooks. A new toilet seat costs $20 to $30 and screws on in ten minutes, a showerhead costs $20 to $35 and twists on by hand, and knobs and hooks cost $15 to $25 total.

Start with the showerhead, because a better showerhead changes every single shower. Choose a handheld model if you like to rinse the tub, or a wide rain head if you want a gentler spray. Wrap the threads with plumber's tape, hand-tighten the new head, and keep the old one in a bag so you can swap it back when you move. Check the water pressure before you buy, because a wide head on low pressure feels weaker than the original.

Next, swap the cabinet knobs and the towel hooks for matching pieces in one finish, such as matte black or brushed nickel. Mixed finishes look accidental, while one matching finish looks planned. A matching set of knobs costs $12 to $20 for a small vanity, and a towel ring or hook costs $8 to $12. Finish with fresh towels in one color, rolled in a basket instead of folded on a shelf, and the bathroom will look like a hotel room for about $30.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a small bathroom makeover cost?

A small bathroom makeover costs $50 to $100 for a renter-friendly refresh, or $200 to $500 if you replace the mirror, the faucet, and the light fixture as well. At the $100 level, spend on the mirror, lighting, hardware, shower curtain, and towels, because those are the pieces your eyes land on first. Skip anything that needs a plumber, and keep the old parts to restore the room when you move.

Can I do a bathroom makeover in a rental?

Yes, as long as every change is removable and your lease allows it. A small bathroom makeover in a rental works best with swaps that need no drilling: new knobs, a new showerhead, a framed mirror, peel-and-stick accents, and fresh textiles. Keep the original parts in a bag, take before photos, and ask your landlord in writing before you paint or change a fixture.

What is the cheapest way to update a small bathroom?

Start free by clearing the counter and the shower ledge, then spend $25 on a new shower curtain and bath mat. Those two pieces set the color for the whole room and cost less than any fixture. Next, spend $15 on warm bulbs and $15 on matching knobs, and the bathroom will feel new for about $55 total, with no tools beyond a screwdriver.

How do I make a small bathroom look expensive on a budget?

Use one finish for all the hardware, one color for all the towels, and one large mirror instead of many small decorations. Matching matte black or brushed nickel knobs, hooks, and a towel ring look planned, while mixed finishes look accidental. Add a small plant, a tray for the soap, and rolled towels in a basket, and the room will read as calm and finished rather than crowded.

Should I replace the toilet seat in a rental bathroom?

Yes, if the old seat is stained or wobbly, because a new toilet seat costs $20 to $30, screws on in ten minutes, and makes the whole bathroom feel cleaner. Keep the old seat in a bag in the closet so you can put it back when you move out. Choose a soft-close seat in white, which matches almost any toilet and closes quietly at night.
