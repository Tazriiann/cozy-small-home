12 Small Balcony Decor Ideas on a Budget

These small balcony decor ideas on a budget turn a concrete slab with a railing into a second room — a morning coffee spot, an evening reading nook, or a tiny garden — for about $50 to $150 total. Most apartment balconies are barely big enough for two chairs, so the trick is to keep everything light, foldable, and weather-proof, and to let plants and light do most of the decorating. Every idea below is renter-friendly, needs no drilling, and comes inside when the season ends. If the balcony doubles as your dining spot in summer, these small dining area ideas for apartments show how a small table can seat two to four people indoors too.

Small Balcony Decor: How Do I Start With a Bare Balcony?

Start with the floor, because a bare concrete balcony feels like a rooftop, while a rug or a set of deck tiles makes it feel like a room. An outdoor rug about four feet by six feet costs $25 to $40, and interlocking wood or plastic deck tiles cost $20 to $40 for a small balcony, and both go down without tools and come up when you move. Sweep the floor first, lay the rug or tiles flat, and keep the edges away from the drain so rainwater can still run off.

Add two chairs and a small table next, because a balcony with nowhere to sit never gets used, no matter how pretty it looks. A pair of folding chairs and a small side table costs $40 to $70, and folding furniture can come inside during a storm or in winter, so it lasts longer. Keep the furniture light enough to carry with one hand, because you will move it every time you clean the balcony.

How Do I Choose Furniture for a Small Balcony?

Choose furniture that folds, stacks, or hangs, because a small balcony has no room for a piece that only does one job and never moves. A folding bistro set, a hanging rail table, and a pair of stackable chairs all fit a balcony about four feet deep, and each one costs $25 to $70 at IKEA, Target, Amazon, or Walmart.

Measure the balcony before you buy, and leave at least two feet of clear walkway between the furniture and the door, so you can step outside without climbing over a chair.

1. A Folding Bistro Set — About $50 to $70

A bistro set with two folding chairs and a small round table seats two people for coffee or dinner, and it folds flat against the wall when you need the floor. Choose metal or resin, because wood needs more care outdoors and fabric cushions soak up rain.

2. A Hanging Rail Table — About $25 to $40

A table that hooks over the balcony railing gives you a surface for coffee or a laptop without taking any floor space at all. It works best on a balcony too narrow for a freestanding table, and it comes off in seconds.

3. A Storage Bench or Deck Box — About $40 to $60

A small waterproof box or a bench with storage inside holds cushions, plant tools, and string lights, so the balcony stays tidy and the cushions stay dry. Keep it against the wall, and use the lid as an extra seat when guests come over.

4. Floor Cushions and a Low Table — About $30 to $50

A pair of outdoor floor cushions and a low table turn a tiny balcony into a lounge without any chairs at all. It suits a balcony where chairs feel too tall, and the cushions stack inside when it rains.

How Do I Add Light to a Small Balcony at Night?

Use battery-powered or solar string lights, because a balcony has no outlet and no wiring, and a string of warm lights turns a concrete box into a room after dark. Solar string lights cost $15 to $25, charge all day.

Switch on by themselves at dusk, while battery-powered fairy lights cost $8 to $15 and run for weeks on one set of batteries. Drape the lights along the railing or across the ceiling, and keep the battery box in a dry spot under a chair or inside a small box.

Add one lantern or a battery-powered candle on the table, because a single warm light at table height makes the balcony feel like a cafe instead of a porch. A lantern costs $10 to $15, runs on batteries or a candle, and comes inside when the weather turns. The same lighting tricks work indoors too — see these renter-friendly lighting ideas for small rooms for lamps and sconces that need no wiring at all.

Small Balcony Decor: What Plants Survive on a Balcony?

Choose plants that match your sun, because a sunny balcony burns shade plants and a shady balcony starves sun plants, and the right plant in the right spot needs almost no care. Herbs like basil, mint, and rosemary grow well in pots on a sunny balcony, while ferns, pothos, and snake plants survive in shade, and all of them cost $5 to $15 per pot at a garden center or a grocery store. Use light plastic pots instead of heavy ceramic ones, so you can move them inside in winter, and put a tray under each pot to catch water before it drips to the neighbor below.

Tip: Check your lease and your building rules before you hang anything on the railing, because some buildings forbid planters on the outside of the rail for safety. Hang planters on the inside of the rail instead, or keep pots on the floor and on a small shelf, so nothing can fall to the street below.

Water in the morning, because plants watered in the morning dry out during the day and do not sit wet overnight, which stops mold and mosquitoes. A small watering can by the door reminds you to water when you step outside, and a weekly check for dry soil is enough for most balcony plants in summer.

How Do I Make a Small Balcony Feel Private?

Block the view, not the light, because a balcony feels private when you cannot see the neighbors, but it feels like a cave when you block the sun too. A bamboo screen, an outdoor fabric panel, or a row of tall plants along the railing all give privacy without drilling, and each one costs $20 to $40.

A fabric panel tied to the railing with zip ties or rope comes down cleanly when you move, while tall plants in pots do the same job and look better.

Keep the screen below the top of the railing if your building has rules about rail height, and leave a gap at the bottom for air and for rainwater to run off. A screen on one side only is usually enough, because most balconies face one neighbor, not three, and the open sides keep the breeze and the light.

What Should I Buy First for a Small Balcony on a Budget?

Buy an outdoor rug, two folding chairs, and a string of solar lights first, because those three pieces turn any balcony into a usable room for about $50 to $100 total. The rug makes the floor feel like a room, the chairs give you a place to sit.

The lights make the balcony usable after dark, which is when most people actually have time to sit outside. Add a small table and two or three plants next, then a storage box only if cushions and tools start piling up.

Shop at the end of summer, because balcony furniture goes on sale in August and September, and a bistro set that costs $70 in May often costs $35 to $40 in September. If the balcony table ends up inside in winter, our small living room makeover on a $200 budget shows where it can live indoors. Measure the balcony, check the building rules about rail planters and screens, and buy only what fits the measurements — furniture that blocks the door will never get used, no matter how nice it looks.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I decorate a very small balcony?

Small balcony decor starts with the floor, because a rug or deck tiles make a tiny balcony feel like a room before you buy anything else. Add two folding chairs and a small table, then string lights and two or three plants, and stop there — a balcony about four feet deep holds a bistro set and a few pots, and anything more makes it feel crowded instead of cozy. These small balcony decor ideas stop at the basics on purpose, because a tiny balcony only holds a bistro set and a few pots.

What furniture fits on a small apartment balcony?

Folding, stackable, and hanging furniture fits best, because it can come inside during a storm or in winter. A folding bistro set, a hanging rail table, and a pair of stackable chairs all fit a balcony about four feet deep, as long as you leave two feet of clear walkway between the furniture and the door. Measure the balcony before you buy, and choose metal or resin over wood for easy care.

Can I hang things on my balcony railing in a rental?

Check your lease and your building rules first, because some buildings forbid anything on the outside of the railing for safety. Hang planters and screens on the inside of the rail instead, or keep pots on the floor and on a shelf, so nothing can fall. Removable hooks and zip ties come off cleanly, but a planter that falls from a balcony is a safety problem, not just a decor problem.

What plants are best for a small balcony?

Match the plant to your sun: herbs like basil, mint, and rosemary grow well on a sunny balcony, while ferns, pothos, and snake plants survive in shade. Use light plastic pots with trays underneath, water in the morning, and bring the pots inside in winter. Start with two or three pots, and add more only when the first ones are thriving.

How much should I budget for a small balcony makeover?

Most renters can set up a working balcony for $50 to $150, spread over a few weeks. Start with a rug, two chairs, and string lights for about $50 to $100, then add a table and plants for $25 to $50 more. Shop end-of-summer sales for furniture, and buy plants at a grocery store or a garden center instead of a boutique nursery.
