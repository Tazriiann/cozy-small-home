10 Small Bathroom Storage Ideas Under $25

Affiliate disclosure: This post may contain affiliate links. If you buy through a link, we may earn a small commission at no extra cost to you. Prices are in US dollars and can change — always check the current price before you buy.

Small bathroom storage under $25 is easier to find than most renters expect, because almost every fix is a small, removable piece that costs less than a takeout dinner. Most of the clutter comes from the same few problems: no counter space, a crowded shower, and cabinets that are too deep or too shallow to hold everything neatly. Each idea below costs under $25, needs no drilling and no permanent changes, and uses simple pieces from IKEA, Target, Amazon, and Walmart. Pick two or three that solve your biggest daily annoyance, and your bathroom will start to feel bigger within an afternoon. The same renter-friendly approach works across your whole home — see these small apartment storage ideas for more rooms.

How Do I Free Up Shower, Sink, and Counter Space?

Start where you get ready every day, because a clear sink and a tidy shower make the whole bathroom feel calmer. A suction caddy keeps bottles off the tub edge, a small tray gathers your daily items into one neat zone, and an over-door towel bar gives hand towels a home without taking any counter space.

The five ideas below all cost under $25, install in minutes, and come down cleanly when you move.

1. Suction Shower Caddy — About $18

A suction shower caddy keeps shampoo, conditioner, and body wash off the tub edge and within easy reach. Look for one with strong suction cups and drainage holes, so water does not pool and bottles do not slide around. Press it firmly onto clean, smooth tile or glass, and test it with full bottles before you trust it overnight. It holds two or three bottles neatly, which is usually enough for one or two people sharing a shower. When you move, peel it off, rinse the cups, and use it in your next bathroom.

2. Over-Cabinet-Door Towel Bar — About $10

An over-the-door towel bar hooks over a cabinet door or a bathroom door, with no screws and no damage to your rental. It gives hand towels and washcloths a proper home instead of draped over the shower rod or piled on the counter. Choose a slim bar that lets the door close fully, and keep it near the sink so towels are easy to grab. It also helps towels dry faster because air can move around them. At about $10, it is one of the cheapest ways to free up counter space.

3. Small Countertop Tray — About $12

A small tray, about $12 at Target or IKEA, turns a scattered sink area into one neat zone. Keep your daily items on it, like a soap dispenser, toothbrush holder, and a small bottle of moisturizer, and store the rest in a cabinet. The tray stops bottles from sliding around and makes cleaning the counter as simple as lifting the tray and wiping underneath. Choose a tray with a raised edge so nothing tips off, and pick a size that leaves room to wash your hands. It works just as well for makeup and skincare.

4. Toothbrush Holder With a Lid — About $9

A covered toothbrush holder keeps brushes upright, separated, and protected from splashes near the sink. Look for one with drainage holes and a lid that lifts off easily for cleaning, and rinse it weekly to keep it fresh. At about $9, it is a small upgrade that makes the sink look much tidier, especially if you share a bathroom. Give each person their own slot, and keep extra brushes and toothpaste in a drawer or bin. A simple holder beats a cup that tips over every morning.

5. Adhesive Hooks for Hair Tools and Robes — About $8

A pack of removable adhesive hooks, about $8, can hold a robe, a hair dryer, or a small basket on the back of the door or on a smooth wall. Stick them where you get ready, so your most-used items are within reach but off the counter. Use hooks rated for the weight you plan to hang, and follow the package directions for removal so the paint stays clean. Hooks are also handy for washcloths, loofahs, and a small hanging bag for travel items. They cost little, install in minutes, and come down cleanly when you move.

How Do I Use Cabinets and Drawers for Small Bathroom Storage?

Once the sink is clear, use the storage you already have more cleverly, with bins, trays, and a rolling cart. Clear bins turn a deep cabinet into pull-out zones for hair care, skincare, and first aid, while a drawer tray keeps small items in neat lanes and a slim cart adds shelves beside the toilet.

For pantry-style bins in the kitchen, the same clear-bin habit works — see this guide to small pantry organization ideas for apartments.

6. Stackable Clear Bins — About $15 for a Set

A set of stackable clear bins, about $15, makes a deep bathroom cabinet much easier to use. Use one bin for hair care, one for skincare, and one for first aid, so you can pull out the whole bin instead of digging to the back. Clear sides let you see what is inside at a glance, which stops you buying a second bottle of something you already own. Label the front of each bin if you share the bathroom, and wipe the bins out once a month. They stack neatly when you need more room, and they move with you to your next apartment.

7. Drawer Organizer Trays — About $14

A drawer organizer tray, about $14, turns a jumbled bathroom drawer into neat lanes for makeup, hair ties, nail care, and small tools. Measure the drawer first, then choose an expandable tray or a set of small trays that fit together without sliding around. Keep daily items in the front sections and extras in the back, and empty the tray once a month to toss dried-out products. A tidy drawer saves time every morning because you can see everything in one look. It also stops small items from rolling to the back and getting lost.

8. Slim Rolling Cart — About $24

A slim rolling cart, about $24 from Walmart or Amazon, fits in the narrow gap beside the toilet or vanity and adds three shelves of storage. Use the top shelf for daily items, the middle for extra towels, and the bottom for cleaning supplies or spare toilet paper. The wheels let you pull it out to reach things, then tuck it back out of the way. Choose a cart narrow enough for your space, and keep heavier items low so it stays stable. It is one of the best under-$25 buys for a bathroom with almost no built-in storage.

9. Over-Toilet Shelf or Small Basket Set — About $22

The wall above the toilet is often empty, but a small freestanding shelf or a set of baskets, about $22, can hold towels, extra toilet paper, and a small plant. If you cannot drill, choose a freestanding shelf that stands on the floor, or use baskets on top of the toilet tank lid. Keep the items you reach for most at eye level, and store backups lower down. This spot is also a good place for a small basket of reading material or a spare hand towel. Just keep the shelf light and stable so nothing falls into the toilet.

10. Door-Mounted Hair Tool Holder — About $19

A door-mounted holder for a hair dryer, straightener, and curling iron, about $19, keeps hot tools off the counter and safely stored. It hangs over a cabinet door with no screws, using hooks that fit over the top edge. Let tools cool before you store them, and keep cords neatly wrapped so they do not tangle. This holder is especially helpful in a shared bathroom, where counter space is always in short supply. At under $25, it solves a daily frustration and helps your tools last longer because they are not knocked around.

How Do I Make Small Bathroom Storage Under $25 Stick?

Start with the spot that bothers you most, usually the shower or the sink, and spend $25 to $50 on that first fix. Measure your cabinets, drawers, and the gap beside the toilet before you buy anything, because a perfect organizer in the wrong size is just clutter.

Choose clear bins where you need to see things quickly, and closed baskets where you want a calmer look. Keep receipts for a week in case a piece does not fit, and take your organizers with you when you move, since every idea here is designed to travel from one rental to the next.

If the bathroom still feels cramped after adding storage, a mirror and lighter colors help too — see how to make a small room look bigger without painting.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I add storage to a small bathroom without drilling?

Small bathroom storage in a rental starts with suction caddies, over-door bars and holders, removable adhesive hooks, freestanding shelves, and rolling carts. These options need no screws or holes, come down cleanly when you move, and still add a lot of useful storage. Always check your lease, and use adhesive products made for the surface you are sticking them to.

What is the best first buy for a small bathroom?

Start with the item that fixes your biggest daily annoyance. For most renters, that is a shower caddy, a towel bar, or a set of clear bins for the cabinet. A single $10 to $20 buy can free up counter space and make mornings feel calmer, and you can add more storage later.

How do I keep a small bathroom tidy long term?

Give every item one home, and do a quick five-minute reset each night. Put tools away, wipe the sink, and return towels to their hooks, so clutter never builds up. Once a month, empty one bin or drawer, toss expired products, and put back only what you still use.

What is the best under-$25 storage for a bathroom with no vanity?

A slim rolling cart is usually the best first buy, because it adds three shelves of storage without needing a vanity or a cabinet at all. Park it beside the toilet or in a corner, keep daily items on top and backups below, and roll it out of the way when you clean. Pair it with a suction shower caddy if your shower has no shelf, and you have covered the two biggest gaps for about $40 total.

How do I stop a suction shower caddy from falling off the wall?

Press the suction cups onto clean, smooth tile or glass only, because soap film, grout lines, and textured tile break the seal. Wipe the surface with warm water, dry it, press the caddy firmly, and test it with full bottles before you trust it overnight. If it still slips, try a caddy that also hooks over the showerhead, or move it to a smoother section of tile.
