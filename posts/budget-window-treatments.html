Budget Window Treatments for Small Rooms

These budget window treatments for small rooms prove that the right curtains or blinds can make a window look bigger, a ceiling look taller, and a whole room feel brighter, all for $15 to $60. Window treatments for small rooms work best when they are hung high and wide, made from light fabric, and chosen for the job the room needs: privacy, light, or both. Below are the renter-friendly options that need no drilling, plus the hanging tricks that make cheap curtains look expensive.

What Are the Best Budget Window Treatments for Small Rooms?

The best budget window treatments for small rooms are light, sheer or linen-look curtains on a no-drill rod, because they let in daylight, give you privacy at night, and cost $20 to $40 a pair. Sheer curtains suit living rooms, linen-look curtains suit bedrooms, and a simple roller blind suits a kitchen or bathroom.

Sheer white curtains are the cheapest way to make a small room feel brighter, because they soften harsh sunlight without blocking it, and they cost $15 to $25 a pair at IKEA, Target, or Walmart. Hang them from a tension rod inside the window frame, or from a no-drill adhesive rod above the frame, and they will look like they belong there. Linen-look curtains in cream, pale gray, or soft sage cost $25 to $45 a pair, and they give a bedroom a calm, finished feel while still letting in light.

Roller blinds and Roman shades are better than curtains in kitchens and bathrooms, because they do not collect cooking grease or steam, and they pull up completely to let in full daylight. A simple roller blind costs $15 to $30, fits inside the window frame with a tension mount, and comes down cleanly when you move. Avoid heavy blackout curtains in a small living room, because they make the room feel dark and closed in during the day.

How Do I Hang Curtains in a Rental Without Drilling?

Hang curtains in a rental with a tension rod, a no-drill adhesive rod, or removable hooks, because all three hold a light curtain without holes in the wall. A tension rod fits inside the window frame and costs $8 to $15, while an adhesive rod sticks above the frame and holds up to five pounds.

A tension rod is the simplest option, and it works best for sheer or lightweight curtains in a window with a deep frame. Twist the rod to extend it until it presses firmly against both sides of the frame, then hang the curtain and check that the rod does not slip when you pull the curtain. If the frame is shallow, use a no-drill adhesive rod instead, which sticks to the wall above the window with removable strips rated for the curtain weight.

For heavier linen curtains, use a command-hook rod, which pairs two large removable hooks with a slim curtain rod that rests in the hooks. Hang the hooks high, just below the ceiling, and wide, a few inches past the window on each side, so the curtains frame the window instead of covering it. When you move out, pull the strips down slowly and the wall stays clean. For more no-drill ideas across the whole apartment, see our guide to small apartment decor ideas for renters on a budget.

How High and Wide Should I Hang Curtains in a Small Room?

Hang curtains just below the ceiling and four to six inches wider than the window on each side, because this trick makes the window look larger and the ceiling look taller. Window treatments for small rooms fail most often when the rod sits right above the window frame, which chops the wall in half and makes the window look small.

Measure from the floor to just below the ceiling, then buy curtains that reach the floor or skim it by half an inch. Curtains that stop halfway down the wall make a small room feel unfinished, while floor-length curtains draw the eye up and make the wall feel taller. If your windows are short, floor-length curtains are still the right choice, because the long line of fabric is what creates the height.

When the curtains are open, pull them completely past the window frame, so the whole window shows and the maximum daylight comes in. A curtain that covers a third of the window even when open blocks light and makes the window look smaller. Use tiebacks or simple hooks to hold the panels back during the day, and close them only at night for privacy. How Do I Choose Window Treatments for Small Rooms for Privacy?

Choose window treatments for privacy by matching the treatment to the floor you live on and the direction the window faces. A ground-floor bedroom needs a blind plus a curtain, a high-floor living room needs only a sheer curtain, and a bathroom window needs a frosted film or a top-down blind.

For a bedroom that faces the street, pair a light-filtering roller blind with linen-look curtains, so the blind gives you privacy during the day and the curtains give you darkness at night. For a living room on a high floor, sheer curtains alone are usually enough, because nobody can see in from above. For a bathroom, use a frosted window film, which costs $10 to $15, sticks to the glass with water, and lets in light while blocking the view completely.

Top-down, bottom-up blinds are the best privacy option for a small room, because you can lower the top to let in daylight while the bottom stays closed for privacy. They cost $25 to $50, fit inside the frame with a tension mount, and suit bedrooms and living rooms that face a neighbor. Avoid dark blackout curtains in a room that needs privacy all day, because they trade your privacy for a dark, closed-in room.

What Do Budget Window Treatments Cost?

Budget window treatments for small rooms cost $15 to $60 per window, including the rod, the curtain or blind, and the hanging hardware. Sheer curtains with a tension rod cost $15 to $30, linen-look curtains with an adhesive rod cost $30 to $50, and a roller blind with a tension mount costs $20 to $40.

Buy curtains in pairs, because a single panel looks thin and unfinished, and two panels frame the window properly even when they are closed. Check the curtain length before you buy, because a pair labeled 84 inches suits most apartments with eight-foot ceilings, while 63-inch curtains suit short windows and basement rooms. Keep the receipt and the packaging for a week, so you can return a pair that looks the wrong color in your room light.

If your room still feels dark after new curtains, the problem may be light, not fabric. These renter-friendly lighting ideas for small rooms add lamps and sconces that work with sheer curtains, and indoor plants for small apartments suit a bright window sill once the curtains let the light in. Frequently Asked Questions

What are the cheapest window treatments for a rental?

Sheer curtains on a tension rod are the cheapest window treatments for a rental, at $15 to $30 per window including the rod. Window treatments for small rooms do not need to be expensive to look good, because a light, well-hung sheer curtain makes a room feel brighter than a heavy, expensive one. Buy one pair per window, hang them high and wide, and keep the fabric light.

Can I hang curtains without drilling holes?

Yes, with a tension rod inside the window frame, a no-drill adhesive rod above the frame, or removable hooks that hold a slim rod. These options hold light and medium curtains without holes, and they come down cleanly when you move. Test one hook first, use hardware rated for your curtain weight, and check your lease for rules about adhesive products.

Should curtains touch the floor in a small room?

Yes, curtains should reach the floor or skim it by half an inch in a small room, because floor-length curtains draw the eye up and make the ceiling look taller. Curtains that stop halfway down the wall chop the wall in half and make the window look small. Measure from just below the ceiling to the floor before you buy, and choose 84-inch curtains for most apartments.

Are blinds or curtains better for a small room?

Curtains are better for living rooms and bedrooms, because soft fabric makes a small room feel warmer and taller, while blinds are better for kitchens and bathrooms, because they do not collect grease or steam and they pull up completely. Window treatments for small rooms work best when you match the treatment to the room: fabric where you relax, and a hard blind where you cook or shower.

How do I make cheap curtains look expensive?

Hang them high and wide, use two panels per window, and choose a light, matte fabric with no shine. Cheap curtains look expensive when the rod sits just below the ceiling, the panels reach the floor, and the fabric hangs in soft folds instead of stretched flat. Iron or steam the curtains before you hang them, because wrinkles are the fastest giveaway of a budget pair.
