Best Indoor Plants for Small Apartments

These best indoor plants for small apartments prove that even a tiny room with one window can stay green, because the right plants need little light, little water, and almost no floor space. Indoor plants for small apartments work best when you match the plant to your light, choose a pot that fits the shelf or sill, and water on a schedule you can actually keep. Below are 12 plants that survive real apartments, plus the placement and care tricks that keep them alive.

What Are the Best Indoor Plants for Small Apartments?

The best indoor plants for small apartments are pothos, snake plant, ZZ plant, spider plant, and peace lily, because all five survive low light, forgive missed watering, and fit on a shelf or a windowsill. A pothos trails from a shelf, a snake plant stands tall in a narrow corner, and a ZZ plant thrives in a dark room.

Pothos is the easiest first plant, because it grows in low light, needs water only when the soil feels dry, and tells you when it is thirsty by drooping slightly. A small pothos costs $8 to $15, trails beautifully from a shelf or a hanging planter, and grows new leaves fast enough that you can see progress every week. Snake plant is the best choice for a dark corner, because it stores water in its thick leaves and can go two or three weeks without water. A small snake plant costs $10 to $20 and suits a narrow floor pot beside the sofa.

ZZ plant is the plant for people who forget to water, because its thick stems store water and it survives in a room with almost no natural light. A small ZZ plant costs $12 to $20 and grows slowly, so it stays the same size for months. Spider plant is the best choice for a hanging planter, because its long striped leaves and baby plantlets look great above a desk or a bed, and it costs $8 to $15. Peace lily suits a bathroom, because it loves humidity and flowers in low light, and it costs $12 to $18.

How Do I Choose Indoor Plants for Low Light?

Choose indoor plants for low light by checking how much direct sun your window gets, then picking plants that match it. Indoor plants for small apartments fail most often when a sun-loving plant ends up in a north-facing room, so a snake plant or ZZ plant suits a dark corner, while a pothos or spider plant suits a bright windowsill.

North-facing windows give soft, indirect light all day, which suits snake plant, ZZ plant, pothos, and peace lily. East-facing windows give gentle morning sun, which suits spider plant, pothos, and most herbs. South-facing windows give strong direct sun, which suits succulents, cacti, and a fiddle-leaf fig, but it can scorch a pothos if the plant sits right against the glass. West-facing windows give strong afternoon sun, so keep the plant a foot back from the glass or use a sheer curtain to soften the light.

If your apartment has almost no natural light, use a small grow light, because a $15 clip-on grow light on a shelf turns a dark corner into a plant corner. Run it for eight hours a day on a timer, and keep it six inches above the plant. Where Should I Put Plants in a Small Apartment?

Put plants in a small apartment on windowsills, shelves, and hanging planters, because the floor is the most valuable space in a tiny room and plants belong up high. Indoor plants for small apartments look best when they are grouped in threes on one shelf, not scattered one by one around the room.

Use the windowsill for plants that need the most light, such as herbs, succulents, and small cacti, and use a shelf or a hanging planter for trailing plants such as pothos and spider plant. A hanging planter over a desk or a bed adds green without taking any surface at all, and a macrame hanger costs $10 to $15. Keep one large plant on the floor only if you have a genuinely empty corner, and choose a tall, narrow pot so it takes up as little floor as possible.

In the bathroom, hang a pothos or a peace lily near the shower, because the steam gives them the humidity they love and the bathroom is often the brightest small room in an apartment. In the kitchen, grow herbs on the windowsill, because basil, mint, and parsley need the light and you can snip them while you cook. For the living room, a snake plant beside the sofa and a pothos trailing from a shelf give you green at two heights without crowding the room. How Do I Keep Indoor Plants for Small Apartments Alive?

Keep indoor plants alive in an apartment by watering only when the top inch of soil feels dry, using pots with drainage holes, and giving each plant the light it actually needs. Indoor plants for small apartments die most often from overwatering, not underwatering, because wet soil rots the roots and the plant cannot recover.

Choose pots with drainage holes and a saucer underneath, because a pot without drainage traps water at the bottom and rots the roots within weeks. If you love a pot with no holes, use it as a cover pot and keep the plant in a plastic nursery pot inside it, lifting the plant out to water it and letting it drain before you put it back. Water slowly until a little water comes out of the drainage holes, then empty the saucer so the plant is not sitting in water.

Feed the plants lightly in spring and summer with a half-strength liquid fertilizer once a month, and skip feeding in winter when growth slows. Wipe the leaves with a damp cloth once a month, because dust blocks light and makes the plant look dull. If a plant starts dropping leaves, check the light and the water first, because those two things cause most problems, and move the plant before you buy fertilizer or a new pot.

What Do Indoor Plants Cost for a Small Apartment?

Indoor plants for a small apartment cost $30 to $80 total, with most small plants costing $8 to $20 each. A pothos costs $8 to $15, a snake plant costs $10 to $20, and a ZZ plant costs $12 to $20, while a simple ceramic pot costs $5 to $12 and a bag of potting soil costs $8.

Start with three plants and three pots, because three plants grouped on one shelf look like a display and cost about $40 total, while ten plants scattered around a small room look like a jungle and cost three times as much. Check grocery stores, hardware stores, and local plant shops before you buy online, because a plant you can see in person is less likely to arrive damaged. Ask for a plant that has been in the shop for a week, not one that just arrived, because a settled plant is less likely to drop leaves when you bring it home.

If you want the whole apartment to feel finished, pair the plants with the rest of the room. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the easiest indoor plant for a small apartment?

Pothos is the easiest indoor plant for a small apartment, because it survives low light, needs water only when the soil feels dry, and grows fast enough to see progress every week. Indoor plants for small apartments should start with pothos, because a small pothos costs $8 to $15, trails from a shelf or a hanging planter, and forgives missed watering. Snake plant is a close second for dark corners.

How often should I water indoor plants?

Water indoor plants only when the top inch of soil feels dry, which is usually once a week for most plants and once every two or three weeks for snake plant and ZZ plant. Indoor plants for small apartments die most often from overwatering, so check the soil with your finger before you water. Water slowly until a little drains out, then empty the saucer.

Can plants survive in a room with no windows?

Yes, if you add a small grow light, because a $15 clip-on grow light on a shelf gives a snake plant or ZZ plant enough light to survive in a windowless room. Run the light for eight hours a day on a timer, and keep it six inches above the plant. Without a grow light, even the toughest plants will slowly fade in a room with no natural light at all.

Are indoor plants safe if I have pets?

Some are not, so check before you buy. Peace lily, pothos, and snake plant are toxic to cats and dogs if eaten, while spider plant, Boston fern, and most herbs are safe. Keep toxic plants on a high shelf or in a hanging planter where pets cannot reach them, and choose pet-safe plants for floor pots. Ask the shop or check a pet-safety list before you bring a new plant home.

How many plants should I have in a small apartment?

Three to five plants suit most small apartments, because a group of three on one shelf reads as a display, while ten plants scattered around a tiny room read as clutter. Start with three, learn how they behave in your light, and add more only after the first ones are thriving. One large floor plant plus two small shelf plants is a good balance for a studio or a one-bedroom.
